# Blogdel Monetization Directive

## Status

This document defines the first approved monetization campaigns for Blogdel.

These campaigns are intended to grow alongside the publication while the platform continues improving its editorial quality, sourcing, verification, discovery, distribution, infrastructure, and autonomous operations.

Revenue is an input into the growth of Blogdel. It must not override editorial integrity.

## Core Principles

1. Editorial decisions and commercial decisions must remain separate.
2. Models may operate commercial systems only inside approved rules, inventories, budgets, and permissions.
3. Revenue optimization must not determine what Blogdel presents as true.
4. Commercial placements must be relevant, measurable, and clearly disclosed where required.
5. Reader utility takes priority over unnecessary monetization surfaces.
6. All monetization systems should produce structured data that Blogdel can use to measure performance and improve future decisions.
7. Human-approved commercial inventories are the default. Models select from approved options rather than searching freely for the highest-paying offer.

## Campaign 1: Curated Affiliate Programme

### Objective

Create an affiliate system in which approved programmes, merchants, products, and offers are researched and categorized before models are allowed to use them.

### Operating Model

Affiliate research is a two-stage process.

First, the human operators and research process identify and approve affiliate opportunities. Each approved opportunity is stored in a structured catalogue.

Second, Blogdel models may select the most contextually appropriate affiliate links when publishing or enriching an article, but only from the approved inventory available to that article's category or niche.

### Curated Inventory

Each affiliate record should support fields such as:

- Merchant or programme
- Product or offer
- Destination URL
- Affiliate URL
- Category
- Niche
- Keywords
- Geography
- Eligible article types
- Commission model
- Commission value where available
- Currency
- Start date
- Expiry date
- Disclosure requirements
- Usage restrictions
- Approval status
- Performance data

### Niche Restrictions

Specific niches may only select from explicitly approved affiliate pools.

Examples:

- Food articles may select from approved food, kitchen, dining, cookbook, travel, or related inventories.
- Technology articles may select from approved hardware, software, services, courses, or related inventories.
- Education articles may select from approved books, courses, tools, or learning services.

A model must not insert an unrelated commercial offer simply because it has a higher commission.

### Selection Rules

Affiliate selection should consider:

- Semantic relevance to the article
- Category and niche eligibility
- Reader geography
- Product or service usefulness
- Availability
- Historical click and conversion performance
- Editorial suitability
- Frequency limits

If no approved affiliate link is sufficiently relevant, the article should contain no affiliate placement.

### Measurement

Track at minimum:

- Article
- Category
- Affiliate record
- Placement
- Impressions where measurable
- Clicks
- Conversions
- Gross commission
- Net revenue
- Revenue per article
- Revenue per thousand article views

## Campaign 2: Reader Accounts, Saved Articles, and Email Notifications

### Objective

Build an owned reader relationship by giving users useful reasons to create an account and voluntarily receive relevant notifications.

### Reader Value

Signing up should provide immediate utility.

Initial account features should include:

- Save articles
- Maintain a personal reading library
- Follow categories
- Follow topics
- Follow authors or editorial desks where appropriate
- Request notification when similar articles are published
- Control email preferences
- Unsubscribe or disable specific notification types

Email collection must be tied to clear reader value rather than generic newsletter capture.

### Notification Model

A user may express interest through actions such as:

- Saving an article
- Following a category
- Following a topic
- Enabling notifications for similar coverage
- Subscribing to a recurring briefing

The notification system may then match newly published articles against those preferences.

Models may assist with semantic matching, but delivery must respect explicit user preferences and notification controls.

### Email Products

Potential products include:

- Similar article alerts
- Category alerts
- Daily Blogdel briefing
- Weekly Blogdel briefing
- Topic-specific briefings
- Saved-reading reminders where explicitly enabled

### Measurement

Track at minimum:

- Signups
- Verified email addresses
- Saved articles
- Follows
- Notification opt-ins
- Email sends
- Opens where permitted
- Clicks
- Unsubscribes
- Return visits
- Subscriber conversion by article and category

Reader accounts are both a product feature and part of Blogdel's long-term distribution infrastructure.

## Campaign 3: Blogdel Direct Advertising

### Objective

Create an internal advertising system that allows Blogdel to sell and serve advertisements directly rather than depending entirely on third-party programmatic advertising.

### Initial Operating Model

The first version is managed internally.

Blogdel operators may:

- Contact online businesses
- Approve advertisers
- Create campaigns
- Upload or register creatives
- Select placements
- Set campaign dates
- Set targeting rules
- Set budgets or fixed campaign values
- Monitor delivery and performance

Advertisements may be hard-set to approved placements while the commercial system is still young.

### Future Advertiser Portal

After the internal advertising workflow has been proven, Blogdel may expose a self-service advertiser account system.

A future advertiser may be able to:

- Create an advertiser account
- Create a campaign
- Submit creatives
- Choose eligible categories or niches
- Select dates
- Set a budget
- View delivery
- View impressions and clicks
- Manage billing
- Renew or pause campaigns

Self-service functionality should be built only after real advertiser usage has established which controls and reports are actually needed.

### Advertising Taxonomy

The advertising system should use the same semantic targeting vocabulary as the affiliate and reader-interest systems where practical.

Useful dimensions may include:

- Category
- Niche
- Topic
- Geography
- Article type
- Commercial intent
- Placement
- Audience segment

### Editorial Firewall

Advertisers may purchase distribution or placement.

Advertisers may not purchase favorable editorial conclusions.

The editorial system should not receive instructions to praise, suppress, or alter reporting because of an advertiser relationship.

Sponsored content, if introduced later, must be explicitly identified as sponsored and must remain structurally distinct from ordinary editorial reporting.

### Measurement

Track at minimum:

- Advertiser
- Campaign
- Creative
- Placement
- Category or niche
- Start and end dates
- Budget
- Impressions
- Clicks
- Click-through rate
- Spend
- Revenue
- Effective revenue per thousand impressions
- Campaign status

## Shared Commercial Infrastructure

The first three campaigns should converge on a common structured commercial layer.

A shared taxonomy can support:

- Affiliate eligibility
- Advertisement targeting
- Reader interests
- Email matching
- Category analytics
- Revenue attribution

The publication should eventually be able to connect commercial outcomes to editorial objects without allowing the commercial layer to control editorial truth.

## Economic Measurement

Blogdel should progressively maintain an economic record for each article and category.

Useful measures include:

- Production cost
- Model cost
- Image cost
- Infrastructure cost
- Pageviews
- Returning readers
- Saves
- Subscriber conversions
- Affiliate revenue
- Advertising revenue
- Sponsorship revenue
- Net contribution

These measures are intended to help Blogdel understand the economics of the publication and allocate future resources intelligently.

They are not an instruction to maximize revenue at the expense of editorial quality, reader trust, or long-term platform strength.

## Current Priority

These are the first three approved monetization campaigns:

1. Curated affiliate programme
2. Reader accounts with saved articles and email notifications
3. Blogdel direct advertising

Other monetization models may be researched later, including sponsorships, paid intelligence, subscriptions, syndication, APIs, marketplaces, and licensing.

They are not part of the initial monetization campaign set unless added by a future directive.
